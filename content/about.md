Strange Canada is a roadside oddities atlas — a field guide to the giant perogies, landing pads built for visitors who never came, smiling rocks, crooked bushes, and preserved ghost towns scattered across this enormous country.

Most of these places were built by small towns, for small towns: a chamber of commerce with a welding shop, a centennial committee with big dreams, a community that decided its story deserved to stand twenty feet tall beside the highway. We think they were right. This site exists to celebrate them — affectionately, always, and never at their expense.

## How we work

- **We check that things are still standing.** Every attraction page carries a "Still Standing" stamp with the date we last confirmed it. Places that come down don't get deleted — they get a memorial page, because the story still matters.
- **Claims are labelled as claims.** When a town calls its lobster the world's largest, we print it as the town's claim and tell you who else is claiming the same crown. That's half the fun.
- **Art, not scraped photos.** Our illustrations are drawn for this site in a vintage postcard style. We don't lift other people's travel photos.
- **Hours are never guessed.** Where visiting details change with the seasons, we say so and point you to the town or attraction for the latest.

## Our sister site

Strange Canada catalogues the odd places. Our sister site [Tales of Canada](https://talesofcanada.ca/) tells the full stories behind places like them: long, sourced accounts of the events, disasters, inventions, and lives that shaped this country, each one published with its sources on the page. When one of our roadside stops leaves you wanting the whole story, that is where it lives.

## Who's behind it

Strange Canada is researched, written, and published from Winnipeg, Manitoba, and is part of the Dark Haus Studio family of Canadian sites. Road trips are planned the proper way: a full tank, a paper map for backup, and a rule that you always, always stop for the giant thing.

Spotted an oddity we haven't covered, or know one on our list has changed? [Tell us about it](/contact/) — reader tips are how half of these places get found.
