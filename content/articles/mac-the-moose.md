Every city on the Trans-Canada Highway has the same problem: a river of travellers flowing past at highway speed, and about four seconds to convince any of them to stop.

In 1984, Moose Jaw, Saskatchewan found its answer. It built a moose you can see from the next postal code.

## Thirty-Four Feet of Welcome

Mac the Moose stands on the grounds of the city's visitor centre, at the corner of Thatcher Drive and the Trans-Canada Highway, where he has been greeting the traffic for four decades. He is a steel frame wrapped in wire mesh and skinned in concrete — roughly ten tons of him — and he was named the way all good prairie decisions get made: the town held a vote, and Mac, in honour of local alderman Les MacKenzie, won.

For thirty-one years, Mac held the title Moose Jaw proudly claims for him: the world's tallest moose statue, at 9.8 metres. Nobody challenged it. Honestly, nobody else was playing.

Then Norway started playing.

## The Moose War

In 2015, the municipality of Stor-Elvdal, Norway unveiled Storelgen — the Big Elk — a mirror-polished steel moose gleaming in a mountain valley. He was beautiful. He was modern. And he was about 30 centimetres taller than Mac.

Moose Jaw took it the way Canadians take most insults: politely, and then increasingly less politely. For four years Mac was billed, through gritted teeth, as the world's *second* largest moose. Then, in January 2019, a pair of Canadian comedians made a video asking the question the whole country was suddenly asking: are we really going to let Norway win by a foot?

The town was not. Suggestions rolled in — a hat, a helmet, stilts — before the obvious answer won out. Moose grow new antlers every year. Mac would simply grow his.

## New Antlers, Old Crown

In March 2019 the two towns declared a Moose Truce — Moosehead Breweries chipped in a $25,000 donation to seal it — and that October, a crane crew fitted Mac with a new set of antlers: bigger than the old ones, and lighter, so his neck could carry the ambition. His height rose to 10.36 metres — 34 feet — and Moose Jaw reclaimed the tallest-moose crown it had lent out for four years.

The peace terms were generous. Norway's Storelgen was granted, in perpetuity, the undisputed title of world's shiniest and most attractive moose. Both moose-ipalities declared themselves satisfied. Mac's old antlers were set at the base of the statue, where they remain — a retired champion's trophies, kept close.

> Thirty centimetres started it. Two towns, one brewery, and a new set of antlers ended it — the friendliest arms race in roadside history.

## Worth the Stop

Stand at Mac's hooves at golden hour, when the prairie light does its long evening stretch and the whole statue glows, and the joke dissolves into something better. Mac isn't really about height. He's about a city that decided the traffic shouldn't just pass — it should be *welcomed*, by the biggest, friendliest greeter Saskatchewan could build.

Norway got the shine. Moose Jaw kept the skyline.
