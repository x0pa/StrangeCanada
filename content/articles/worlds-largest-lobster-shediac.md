Every fishing town on the Atlantic coast will tell you, over a properly buttered roll, that *its* lobster is the best in the world. Shediac, New Brunswick simply decided to settle the argument in concrete.

Drive into town from the west and there it is: a lobster the size of a bus, bright red and rearing over the harbour on a great stone pedestal, claws raised like it's delighted to see you. This is the World's Largest Lobster — Shediac's official greeter, landmark, and hometown argument made permanent.

## Eleven Metres of Hometown Pride

The big lobster was commissioned by the Shediac Rotary Club in 1989 and completed in 1990, the creation of New Brunswick artist Winston Bronnum. Bronnum spent three years on it, building the creature out of reinforced concrete — which is how a sculpture ends up with these numbers: about 11 metres long, 5 metres wide, 5 metres tall, and, by the figures commonly reported, around 90 tonnes all in, pedestal included. The lobster alone accounts for roughly 55 of those tonnes.

And Bronnum didn't build a cartoon. The statue is a faithful portrait of the real animal — segmented tail, long antennae, and those two magnificent, mismatched claws — just scaled up until a whole town could stand in its shadow. Fittingly, a staircase winds up the pedestal, so visitors can climb up, stand beside the giant, and get the photograph that has launched a thousand fridge doors.

## Why a Lobster?

Because in Shediac, the lobster isn't a mascot. It's the economy, the history, and most of the menu.

This is a working Acadian fishing town on the Northumberland Strait, and the lobster fishery has shaped it for generations — the boats in the harbour, the traps on the wharves, the suppers on the tables. The monument, for its part, is reported to draw some 500,000 visitors a year, which would make this concrete crustacean one of the hardest-working greeters in the Maritimes. Shediac proclaims itself the Lobster Capital of the World, and whatever other ports may think of that claim, Shediac has done something none of them can argue with: it built the claim a monument, at a scale visible from the highway.

> Other towns say it. Shediac poured it in concrete.

## Come in July

Time your trip for the town's Lobster Festival, held each July, and the statue stops being the biggest lobster story in Shediac. The harbour fills with boats dressed for celebration, the wharves fill with people, and the creature on the pedestal gets served the proper way at long tables all over town — boiled red, cracked open, and eaten in the sunshine a short walk from where the fleet ties up.

It's the same secret the prairie giants keep: the statue gets you off the highway. The festival makes you stay for supper.

## Worth the Drive

Stand at the foot of the pedestal in the late light, when the lobster glows red against the harbour, and the statue reads exactly as it was meant to: not a joke, but a thank-you. A fishing town looked at the animal that fed it, clothed it, and put it on the map — and said, *that, but bigger.*

Then it built a staircase, so you could climb up and say hello.
