Aspen trees are not complicated. An aspen finds a patch of prairie, sends up a straight white trunk, and gets on with the business of trembling in the wind. All over Saskatchewan, they do exactly that, in straight, sensible rows.

Then there is one small bush near Hafford where they do nothing of the kind.

## What Is the Crooked Bush?

The Crooked Bush — also called the Crooked Trees, the Twisted Trees, or the Crooked Trees of Alticane — is a grove of trembling aspens covering about 3 acres (1.2 hectares) in the Rural Municipality of Douglas, about 20 kilometres north-northwest of Hafford and just over 5 kilometres southwest of Alticane. It sits on the southern slopes of the Thickwood Hills, within the Redberry Lake Biosphere Reserve.

Inside that small patch, nothing grows straight. Trunks corkscrew, branches loop sideways and reach downward, and whole trees tie themselves into knots on their way to a modest 15 to 20 feet tall. The Friends of the Crooked Bush call it a botanical mystery, and CBC's *Morningside* once named it one of the 54 Wonders of Canada. Local residents first identified the grove in the 1940s, and it has been quietly bending ever since.

The sharpest detail is at the edge of the road. Less than 10 metres away, other aspens grow perfectly straight, the way aspens are supposed to. Whatever happened here, happened to this one bush, and to nothing else around it.

## Why Do the Trees Grow Crooked?

The best answer science has is written in the trees themselves. The Town of Hafford reports that scientists believe the growth is the result of a genetic mutation, though they are unsure what caused the mutation. The strongest clue came from cuttings: trees propagated from this grove in Manitoba grew with the same twisted pattern, far from Hafford's soil, which points to genetics rather than anything in the ground.

Aspens also spread by root suckers, sending up new trunks from a shared root system. That means the whole crooked grove is likely a family of clones, all descended from a single mutated tree, each new trunk faithfully repeating the original's strange instructions. The trees grow at a normal rate, the town notes. Their branches just grow in every direction other than straight up.

That may also be why the bush survives at all. A crooked aspen competing with straight, fast-growing neighbours for sunlight would lose. In this one sheltered patch, bounded by grid roads, the grove has had room to be itself — and its size is now essentially fixed, unlikely to spread further.

## What Do the Local Legends Say?

Where science leaves a gap, Saskatchewan folklore happily moves in. The Town of Hafford records the local legends plainly, as legends: the twisting has been attributed to everything from a UFO landing to a lightning strike that affected the area's soil. The legends also claim that people who enter the area experience dizziness, and that, despite the lack of a fence, cattle will not travel through the trees.

Those stories are reported here the way the town tells them — as reported accounts, part of the folklore that has grown up around the grove, with no verdict offered either way. What is certain is simpler and strange enough on its own: the trees are real, the twist is in their genes, and nobody knows what flipped the switch in the 1940s, or long before anyone noticed.

> Every other tree on this road grows straight up. In one small bush, up was never the point.

## Worth the Stop

The Crooked Bush asks very little of you. It is privately owned but open to the public, with no permission or appointment needed, and a short boardwalk carries you through the grove while protecting the new growth underfoot. The town's request is the whole of the etiquette: stay on the boardwalk, do not climb the trees or break branches, and leave nothing behind.

In return, you get the rarest thing in the atlas: a roadside wonder nobody built. No committee raised it, no fundraiser paid for it, and no rival town can build a crookeder one. A single aspen took a different path sometime before anyone was watching, and its whole family has been gracefully, impossibly bent ever since.

Drive the grid roads north of Hafford, follow the signs from Flint Road, and stand on the boardwalk a while. The straight trees across the road will be doing their sensible best. These ones will be doing something else entirely.
